### 03 · A Mountain Day Out

Pine trees, mountain air, and a little time away from town.

Near Lingyan Mountain, visitors gather beneath the trees while attendants prepare food. Some travelers even have a carrying chair nearby. Apparently, a scenic day out could involve both a picnic and a ride!

### 04 · A Bridge into Business

A bridge, a river, and shops right beside the water.

Researchers describe this part of Mudu as a busy commercial district. Cross the bridge and you're among the storefronts; arrive by boat and the market is close by, too.

Two ways to get there, plenty of reasons to stop.

### 05 · A Temple Behind the Shops?

Look past the riverside shops. See that little six-sided pavilion?

Researchers identify it as part of a temple compound tucked behind Mudu's busy storefronts. The same neighborhood had room for both trade and religious life.

Even a lively shopping street could have a quieter neighbor.

### 06 · Wedding Traffic, On Water?

Bright decorations and a very crowded boat—could this be a wedding?

Researchers interpret the boats near Mudu as a wedding procession. In a town threaded with waterways, a celebration could travel by river.

### 07 · A Granary with a Backup Plan

Those neat rows of rooftops hint at a less glamorous side of prosperity.

Researchers identify this waterside compound as a *shecang*, or community granary. Granaries like these stored grain to help communities cope with shortages and poor harvests.

Think of it as a community backup pantry—on a much bigger scale.

### 08 · A Garden Party, Qing-Style

A garden, an elegant hall, and plenty of company.

This Mudu residence has planted courtyards, ornamental rocks, and rooms where people gather. Researchers suggest it may be the historic Suichu Garden, though that identification remains uncertain.

Some gardens were made for enjoying company as much as scenery.

### 09 · A Bridge with Nine Arches

Nine arches—and, according to tradition, nine moons.

This is Xingchun Bridge at Stone Lake. On certain autumn nights, people came to see moonlight shine through its arches and reflect on the water, a sight called *Shihu Chuanyue*.

Xu Yang painted the bridge by day, but its famous spectacle belonged to the night.

### 10 · Farming at the Water's Edge

Not every quiet patch of water was empty space.

Look at the low banks dividing the shore into plots. Researchers describe fields and fishponds around Stone Lake, where people made productive use of the watery landscape. Even this peaceful stretch had work going on between the waves.

### 11 · Showtime in Old Suzhou

The crowd has found something more exciting than shopping.

Look at the colorful stage and all the spectators gathered around it. The scene offers a glimpse of live entertainment in Qing-era Suzhou. Long before anyone could stream a show, a performance could draw quite a crowd.

### 12 · A City in Full Color

Those colorful lengths of fabric have a story behind them.

Suzhou was a major textile-dyeing center, and a 2026 exhibition report notes four dye works across the entire scroll. Here, bright cloth hangs outside a workshop. The city's colorful fashions didn't dye themselves!

### 13 · A Pagoda Above the Rooftops

Follow the city wall, then look up. Spot the pagoda?

Researchers identify this part of the scroll as Panmen, with Ruiguang Pagoda rising behind the fortifications. One landmark belonged to the city's defenses; the other was part of a Buddhist temple. Two very different histories in one skyline.

### 14 · Where the Boats Met the Street

All those boats needed somewhere to land.

Researchers identify this busy waterfront as Xumen wharf. Stone embankments and steps linked the water to the crowded shore, where boats gathered near the city wall. For passengers and goods alike, this was where river journeys met city streets.

### 15 · A Bridge in Two Colors

Stone railings here, red-painted wooden ones there—look closely!

Researchers identify this as Wannian Bridge near Xumen. Its parapets combined stone and red wooden sections, creating an unusual alternating pattern.

It wasn't just a way across the river: the bridge had a little visual flair, too.

### 16 · Tea with a Name

A museum close-up reveals a tiny sign: *Songluo Tea Room* (松蘿茶室).

Songluo is the name of a celebrated green tea associated with the Huangshan region. Tea drinking was part of Suzhou's social life, and this little sign hints at a much bigger story of taste and trade. Fancy a cup?

### 17 · Flags Mean Official Business

Those tall flags aren't advertising another shop.

Researchers identify this compound as the Jiangsu provincial administration office. Provincial officials handled matters such as taxation and grain administration—a very different kind of business from the markets outside. Suzhou's prosperity came with paperwork, too.

### 18 · Signs, Signs Everywhere!

Look at the shop signs lining this busy street near Changmen.

Recent exhibition reporting counts more than 200 business signs across the *whole* scroll—not just this street.

The characters may be too small to read here, but the message is clear: Suzhou's shopkeepers knew how to compete for attention.

### 19 · A City Gate with a Crowd

A city gate, a bridge, and hardly any room to stand still!

This is Changmen, a major gateway along Xu Yang's painted route through Suzhou. Beyond the defensive walls, boats, shops, and pedestrians crowd together. A city gate could be a landmark—and a lively meeting place.

### 20 · A City with Two Kinds of Streets

Why choose between roads and canals when you can have both?

Around Shantang, boats travel beside busy streets, linking shops and landing places in different ways. This side-by-side network of water routes and walkways was a defining feature of Suzhou's urban life. Two kinds of traffic, one bustling neighborhood.

### 21 · Room for Boats, Too

People cross above. A covered boat passes underneath.

Along Shantang's waterways, arched bridges linked streets while leaving space for river traffic below. Notice the raised opening and the people crossing overhead.

One bridge, two streams of traffic—and neither had to turn around.

### 22 · A Tower at the Finish Line

After all those markets, bridges, and boats, the scroll ends on a hill.

This is Tiger Hill, where a pagoda rises above temple buildings. Xu Yang's journey has taken us from countryside to busy city streets and finally to this landmark. Quite a long walk—for your eyes, at least.
